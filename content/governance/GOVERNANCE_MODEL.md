## 0. What this document is

This is the **governance model for the six-month organic pilot**: who may draft content, who approves it, how it gets published, what happens to rejected drafts, what brand and sourcing rules bind every post, and how the model hands off to in-house staff across Months 3–6.

It is a **framework with per-store application**, never a rigid template forced on five autonomous rooftops. Each GM decides whether and how their store participates (voluntary adoption, no top-down mandate). Every store-specific field is a blank marked **TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil**. Nothing about the stores is assumed or invented.

The single rule that governs everything: **nothing is published, sent, or spoken to anyone at Greenway without Shannon McNeil's approval.** She is the only client-facing voice and the only operator of Sprout Social.

## 1. Where governance sits in the workflow

```
audit findings → content pillars → calendar → creative briefs → copy → APPROVAL → publish → measure
 (audit/)        (STRATEGY.md)    (MONTH-XX.md)  (briefs/)      (draft)   (this file)  (Sprout,  (reporting/)
                                                                           + WORKFLOW.md  Shannon only)
```

1. **Governance gates the calendar pipeline.** Every item on a store's calendar (`content/calendars/store-XX/MONTH-XX.md`) moves through the statuses in `CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE.md` §1: `Draft` → `Lead review` → `Approved` / `Edits needed`. The approval tracker in the calendar (§5) is the working record of this model in action.
2. **Governance does not change the pillars, mix, or cadence.** Those come from the store's strategy (`content/strategies/store-XX/STRATEGY.md` per `CONTENT_STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md`). Governance controls who handles each piece and what rules bind it.
3. **Governance does not invent measurement.** What gets measured comes from `reporting/GM_KPI_FRAMEWORK.md`; governance ensures the content that feeds those numbers was approved and sourced honestly.

## 2. Who may create what (roles and drafting rights per rooftop)

Content for a store's pages is drafted by the **Content Strategist** (this team) during the pilot. In-house roles join across Months 3–6 as the store trains up (see §9 and `TRAIN_THE_TRAINER_PLAYBOOK.md`).

| Role | May draft | May approve | May publish | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Content Strategist (Bright Matter) | All six channels: posts, captions, scripts, GBP review responses, Reddit participations | No | No | Drafts every piece per its creative brief |
| Reporting Analyst / Audit & Research Analyst | Review-response language and factual context only, via briefs | No | No | Feed content, never publish |
| Paid Media Specialist | Nothing for organic pilot pages | No | No | Paid is out of scope (see §5.4) |
| Team lead | No (reviews only) | Reviews all drafts before Shannon | No | Gate 2 of the pipeline |
| Shannon McNeil | Yes, at her discretion | **Yes — final and only approval** | **Yes — only publisher via Sprout** | Human-operated publishing; see §3 |
| Store GM | No (approves store participation, not posts) | No (see §3 note) | No | Owns adoption decision and one-on-one conversations |
| Store content lead / trainee (Months 3–6) | Yes, under the progression in `TRAIN_THE_TRAINER_PLAYBOOK.md` | No | No (not until handoff, and only via Shannon-approved workflow) | Co-creates drafts under review |
| Salespeople (personal accounts) | Governed separately by `SALESPERSON_CONTENT_FRAMEWORK.md` | n/a | n/a | Personal accounts are a one-on-one GM conversation, never a group rollout |

**Rules:**
1. One draft per brief. No copy is written until the brief for that calendar slot exists and is complete (`CREATIVE_BRIEF.md`).
2. No agent contacts any store, GM, or the public at any point. All routing goes through Shannon McNeil.
3. Drafting rights are per role, not per person. If a store wants a specific employee to draft (Months 3–6), the name is **TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil** and the role still carries the same limits: draft yes, approve no, publish no.

## 3. The approval chain (the only path to "posted")

```
Step 1  specialist drafts (Content Strategist)
   ↓
Step 2  team lead reviews (accuracy, source-verification, store specificity)
   ↓
Step 3  Shannon McNeil approves (final gate — nothing proceeds without sign-off)
   ↓
Step 4  published by Shannon McNeil only, via Sprout Social (or by the store at her
        explicit direction once the store runs under the handoff plan)
```

1. **Step 1 — specialist drafts.** The Content Strategist produces copy per the store's brief and calendar slot. Drafts live in the workspace as internal files with `Draft` status.
2. **Step 2 — team lead reviews.** The team lead checks every draft for invented facts, missing TO VERIFY markers, tone, per-store specificity, and compliance flags before it goes up. A draft with an unresolved TO VERIFY item does not move.
3. **Step 3 — Shannon McNeil approves.** Her sign-off is recorded in the calendar's approval tracker (`CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE.md` §5) with a date. Nothing is posted before this.
4. **Step 4 — publish.** Shannon publishes through Sprout Social. **Sprout is operated by the human only.** No agent logs into, schedules from, or publishes from Sprout or any store account. If the store posts natively at Shannon's direction (e.g., a delivery-day story shot on a personal phone), the store does so only with her explicit approval and the calendar records it.
5. **No step is skipped, and the order never changes.** A GM does not approve individual posts during the pilot; the GM approves participation, and Shannon approves content. This keeps the pilot's approval single-voiced and traceable.

**Per-store note:** Stores 03 and 04 share a GM (James Galuszka) but are separate rooftops with separate calendars and separate approval records. One GM does not merge two approval trackers.

## 4. Rejected drafts: feedback loop, no silent drops

1. **Every draft gets an outcome.** A draft that is not approved is either `Approved`, `Edits needed`, or `Re-slotted`. It is never dropped silently.
2. **Edits needed means a specific reason.** The reviewer (team lead or Shannon) states what changed in plain language: which fact is unverified, which line is off-tone, which vehicle detail is wrong. The draft returns to `Draft` and re-enters the pipeline (§3).
3. **The calendar row keeps its status.** The approval tracker in `CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE.md` §5 shows the item's full history: `Draft` → `Lead review` → `Edits needed` (date, reason) → `Draft` → `Approved` (date) → `Posted` (date).
4. **Rejected content is not recycled without review.** An item that comes back for edits is rewritten, never re-posted as-is on another channel or another date without re-entering the pipeline.
5. **Patterns get fixed, not papered over.** If edits cluster on one topic (pricing copy, OEM claims), the team lead logs the pattern and the model updates: the relevant guardrail in §5 gets tightened or the TO VERIFY request list grows. One-off fixes hide recurring problems.

## 5. Brand guardrails and OEM compliance

The voice guardrails in `CONTENT_STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md` §4 apply to every piece: honest first, specific beats vague, helpful beats pushy, human not corporate, local first. Compliance adds two layers.

### 5.1 OEM rules — TO VERIFY per brand, never invented

1. **Kia and Ford OEM rules differ, and store rules may differ from OEM rules.** The pilot spans four Kia rooftops and one Ford rooftop. Neither set of rules is known to the team yet.
2. **Every OEM and advertising-compliance rule is TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil** (`CLIENT.md` §8.2: brand guidelines / compliance requirements). This includes: logo and badge usage, model-name and trim styling, incentive and APR phrasing, co-op and trademark rules, and any OEM approval process for social content.
3. **Until a rule is confirmed, the conservative default applies:** no OEM logos or badges beyond the store's own name, no claims about incentives or manufacturer programs, no "certified" or "warranty" language. A default is a placeholder, not a fact — it is replaced the moment Shannon confirms the real rule.
4. **Store 05 (Ford) gets its own compliance pass** even where the Kia stores share rules. One OEM rule never implies the other.

### 5.2 Advertising and offer claims

1. **No price, payment, incentive, or APR figure is published unless Shannon confirms it from the store's real listing or feed.** Every such figure in a draft is marked **TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil** until confirmed.
2. **No "only N left," "act fast," or deadline language unless the limit or deadline is real and confirmed.** Fake urgency is prohibited (`CONTENT_STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md` §4).
3. **No claims about a competitor** — no price comparisons against named dealers, no "better than X" language. Competitor attacks are prohibited outright (see also `SALESPERSON_CONTENT_FRAMEWORK.md` §4).
4. **Sales claims are out.** The pilot measures social and profile activity, not closed sales (`reporting/GM_KPI_FRAMEWORK.md` §5.7). No post claims a sales result the team cannot measure.

### 5.3 Channel-specific guardrails (summary; detail in `CONTENT_STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md` §6)

1. **Reddit** participates, never promotes: no hard-selling, no promotional links, subreddit fit confirmed before any activity, store mention earned by usefulness.
2. **Google Business Profile** review responses are genuine and specific, never templated, within 2 business days. No incentivized reviews (prohibited by Google, per `audit/BENCHMARKS.md` §3.2).
3. **All channels:** real, current, on-brand photos and video; no stock-photo filler; no reposted generic memes.

### 5.4 Out-of-scope boundary

1. **Paid media is not part of this pilot.** No agent launches, funds, modifies, or manages live ad spend, and no agent touches existing Greenway ad accounts (including Facebook paid, where Greenway-side contact Brennan has an informal role) without Shannon's explicit confirmation of expanded scope and access (`OPERATING_RULES.md` §2, hard line). Governance for paid would be built separately, only after that expansion.
2. **Anything delivered beyond the SOW is labeled "included at no charge — pilot goodwill"** and logged in `expansion-signals.md` so it is counted, never silent.

## 6. Content-sourcing rules (nothing invented)

1. **Every vehicle detail is TO VERIFY until confirmed.** Model, trim, year, color, mileage, price, and feature claims in inventory posts come only from the store's real listing or feed via Shannon McNeil. A photo of a vehicle is not proof the details are right; both the photo and the details are confirmed before the draft moves.
2. **No inventory that is not verifiably on the store's lot.** "This week on the lot" means the lot the store actually has, on the week the post runs.
3. **No invented store facts** — hours, address, phone, services, staff names, events, sponsorships. Every one is TO VERIFY until Shannon confirms it.
4. **Staff names and faces** appear only with the employee's consent and Shannon's confirmation (names TO VERIFY). A store employee is never posted about without their knowing participation.
5. **Proof content is real proof.** Reviews quoted or featured are real reviews, captured with a date, and the store has the right to feature them (no fabricating, no quoting a private message as a public review).
6. **Events and community ties are real or they do not post.** A calendar slot that cannot confirm its event is re-slotted, not filled with a placeholder.
7. **Benchmark numbers come from `audit/BENCHMARKS.md` or are labeled house defaults** (`CONTENT_STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md` §9). The team never invents a statistic in a caption.

## 8. Escalation path for sensitive findings

Sensitive findings are handled per `OPERATING_RULES.md` §4: held for **one-on-one GM conversations, never a group setting**, and framed as **absence or opportunity, never a complaint list**.

| Finding type | Examples | Who raises it | How it is raised | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reputation | Negative review patterns, Reddit complaints, fee-transparency questions | Reporting Analyst / Audit & Research Analyst log it; team lead owns the conversation plan | One-on-one with that store's GM, via Shannon McNeil, framed as absence ("the store is not present in the conversation") | Group GM setting, COO readout, group email |
| Staff dependency | Positive reviews concentrated on named individuals; one person runs all posting | Team lead | One-on-one; framed as risk to manage, not a criticism of the staff member | Naming staff in a group setting or in the COO readout |
| Salesperson content | A salesperson's personal post that misstates price, OEM terms, or the store | Team lead via the escalation in `SALESPERSON_CONTENT_FRAMEWORK.md` §7 | One-on-one with that store's GM, opportunity + risk framing, coaching not punishment | Public correction, group rollout, treating it as a compliance scandal |
| Compliance drift | A draft or post that approaches an OEM or ad-practice line | Content Strategist flags in the draft itself | Stops the draft in the pipeline (§4); rule confirmed or changed with Shannon before it moves | Posting anyway, guessing the rule |

**Escalation steps (numbered):**
1. The observing role writes the finding to the internal log (`expansion-signals.md` for expansion-adjacent items, or the audit workbook for reputation items), with evidence and a date.
2. The team lead decides whether it is a one-on-one item, a data-gap item, or a non-item. Sensitive items are one-on-one by default.
3. The team lead prepares the conversation framing with Shannon before any GM meeting: one store, one GM, absence framing, opportunity and risk management, no complaint list.
4. Shannon McNeil raises it with the GM one-on-one. No agent is present and no agent contacts the GM.
5. The outcome (decision, new rule, training need) is recorded internally and, where it changes content rules, folded back into §5 or `SALESPERSON_CONTENT_FRAMEWORK.md`.

## 9. Training the model in-house over Months 3–6

The long-term objective is train-the-trainer: a store employee eventually runs social in-house without Bright Matter (`CLIENT.md` §6). Governance trains them by transferring the **drafting and moderation judgment**, never by transferring approval or publishing keys early.

| Month | In-house role in content | Approval chain during this month | Governance focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 3 — Observe | Trainee watches drafts, briefs, and the calendar; shadows Shannon's approval reviews (where she allows) | Unchanged: Content Strategist drafts, team lead reviews, Shannon approves | Trainee learns the pipeline and the guardrails by seeing them applied |
| Month 4 — Co-create | Trainee proposes topics and drafts alongside the Content Strategist | Unchanged | Trainee's drafts go through the same full pipeline; edits are explained, not just made |
| Month 5 — Own drafts under review | Trainee drafts store content; Content Strategist reviews and edits before team lead review | Content Strategist acts as first reviewer; team lead and Shannon unchanged | Trainee handles routine items start to finish; sensitive items stay with the team |
| Month 6 — Run with agency support | Trainee drafts and schedules under Shannon's direction; Bright Matter reviews weekly, not per post | Shannon approves; team lead spot-checks; Content Strategist reviews weekly batch | Handoff readiness check: can the store sustain cadence, tone, and compliance without per-item agency edits? |

**Rules for the whole progression:**
1. **Approval authority never transfers during the pilot.** Shannon approves until the Month 6 readout, and the store decides what happens after. Nobody on the Bright Matter side hands a publishing key to a store.
2. **Sprout access is Shannon's call.** If a trainee schedules inside Sprout, Shannon grants the limited role and remains the operator of record. Agents never touch Sprout.
3. **The trainee is per store, per GM decision.** One trainee per rooftop (TO VERIFY); Stores 03 and 04 may train separately even under one GM.
4. **Every training step is documented in `TRAIN_THE_TRAINER_PLAYBOOK.md`** — curriculum, weekly rhythm, checklists. Governance and the playbook are two views of the same handoff: the playbook says what the trainee learns, this file says what never leaves the approval chain.
5. **Failure to sustain is a finding, not a fault.** If a store cannot hold cadence or compliance at Month 6, that goes to the GM one-on-one as a capacity question, and the handoff plan stretches rather than pretending readiness.

## 10. Monthly governance checklist (per store)

Run this at the end of every pilot month, alongside the calendar's mix check (`CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE.md` §4):

1. ☐ Every posted item this month has `Approved` by Shannon McNeil in the approval tracker, with a date.
2. ☐ No item was posted with copy status `Draft`, `Lead review`, or `Edits needed`.
3. ☐ Every rejected or edited draft has a recorded reason and a re-entry path (no silent drops).
4. ☐ No published post contained an unverified vehicle, price, offer, event, or store fact. Any post that shipped with a TO VERIFY item is logged as a process miss and reviewed.
5. ☐ Reddit rows that ran had confirmed subreddit fit; no promotional Reddit content was published.
6. ☐ GBP review responses ran within 2 business days and were genuine, not templated.
7. ☐ OEM/compliance items flagged TO VERIFY stayed TO VERIFY until Shannon confirmed them; no rule was guessed.
8. ☐ Sensitive findings raised this month went one-on-one via Shannon, framed as absence/opportunity, never group.
9. ☐ In-house training advanced one month along the §9 progression, or the deviation is recorded with a reason.
10. ☐ Anything delivered beyond scope is labeled "included at no charge — pilot goodwill" and logged in `expansion-signals.md`.

## 11. What this model does not do

1. **It does not police salespeople's personal accounts.** That is `SALESPERSON_CONTENT_FRAMEWORK.md`, introduced one-on-one per GM.
2. **It does not set KPIs or decide what is measured.** That is `reporting/GM_KPI_FRAMEWORK.md`.
3. **It does not price or pitch anything.** Real, observed gaps go to `expansion-signals.md`; the operator decides what gets raised, when, and with which GM (`OPERATING_RULES.md` §2.4).
4. **It does not replace the operator.** Every rule in this file is a draft until Shannon McNeil approves it, and she can change any of them per store at any time.
