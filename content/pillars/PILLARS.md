### 2.1 The format tier layer (NEW — every counted post carries a tier)
- **Tier A — Hero.** Recurring named character, serialized campaign, or **launch walkaround**. Where the ceiling lives; all six-figure posts are Tier A. **Target ≥1/rooftop/week in full mix.**
- **Tier B — Workhorse.** Video, no character required: feature demos, service explainers, comparisons, offer-as-video, delivery-day. The reliable ~2,000-play IG baseline.
- **Tier C — Utility. Published but NOT counted:** per-VIN walkarounds, static offer/compliance graphics, hours/holiday notices, roundup photos, review-milestone graphics. Lives on YouTube/GBP/Facebook.
- **Two hard rules:** every counted social post is Tier A or Tier B (Tier C never fills a mix slot); an offer is published as **video with a person**, or it is not published as a social post.

### 2.2 The five pillars — re-specified (names unchanged)
- **P1 split.** **1a — search asset (Tier C)** per-VIN walkarounds on YouTube/GBP, VIN-coded, judged on search/VDP, **consumes no social mix share**. **1b — social content (Tier A/B)**: launch walkarounds, feature demos, comparisons, offer-as-video.
- **P2 renamed Service & Ownership.** Adds **deal & ownership education** (trade-in, financing, lease-end, first service, "what $0-down means") — the category rev-1 left homeless.
- **P3 People & Character.** Re-specified around a **named person with a running storyline** (the growth engine); not staff intros/"a day at the dealership." Character wins distribution; education wins intent — do not measure P3 on lead volume.
- **P4 Community & Local — GATED.** Every input TO VERIFY at all five rooftops. Opens at 10%, rises to 20% only after that store's local-hook list is verified.
- **P5 split.** **5a — GBP review ops** (named GM KPI + recurring calendar row, **not a post share**). **5b — social proof (Tier A/B video only)**: delivery-day/testimonial video. Post share drops because proof converts on Google, not in an IG carousel.

## 0. What this document is

Five content pillars built to own the stored competitive reality — which is now per-market, **not** a single "field is wide open" claim. On YouTube, across the named external competitors retrievable in the **KC and Nashville** markets (Stores 05, 03/04), the store-level video field is stale (5–16 years old), low-volume, and low-view; no KC or Nashville competitor runs a current, consistent, real inventory or review line. That is CONFIRMED for those markets. **Store 02 (Jacksonville) is the one confirmed exception:** its competitor **Kia on Atlantic (formerly Southside Kia)** runs a current, consistent, near-monthly offer/promo line with 2026-dated content; the rest of the Jacksonville set is stale or under-observed. So the wedge is two-fold and market-specific:
- **KC + Nashville:** competitors are absent or stale → the lane is "post **current, consistent, real** content and own it."
- **Jacksonville (Store 02):** one competitor is current-but-thin → the lane is **depth + utility + local** (a real inventory line, best-seller depth reviews, service/proof, people/local), **not** a price-promo volume race. Store 02 does **not** rely on the "all competitors are stale" argument.

Each pillar below states which success model and which competitor finding ground it, so every pillar is traceable to the evidence and to which market claim supports it.

**Publishing scope honored (CLIENT.md §6a):** the pilot measures all six channels, but **active publishing runs on Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok.** Pillars are therefore designed for FB/IG/TikTok execution first. YouTube and Google Business Profile carry depth, proof, and search roles as supporting surfaces within the same pillars; Reddit is participation only, after subreddit fit is confirmed, and never a broadcast pillar.

**Honesty guardrails carried forward:**
1. The competitor/success observations are ONLY fully CONFIRMED on YouTube. Their **FB/IG/TikTok presence is unobserved (manual verification needed — 2026-08-22).** So "competitors are weak on TikTok/IG" is a **stated hypothesis, not a confirmed claim.** The pillars lead with the CONFIRMED video-persistence wedge and treat cross-platform dominance as an assumption the operator re-checks when FB/IG/TikTok data is retrievable or supplied.
2. Nothing unverifiable: no offer, price, APR, incentive, vehicle, local tie, language split, or named staff member is ever posted without confirmation. Every such item stays **TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil** until the store's real data arrives.
3. Tone guardrails (FRAMEWORK §4): honest first, specific beats vague, no fake urgency, no hype words, human not corporate, local first.
4. Each rooftop is its own market with its own GM and voice. The five pillars are the same five for every store; **which pillars lead, and the specifics under them, change per store** (see §4). No group template where five store-specific deliverables are required.
5. **Hyundai / Kia+Hyundai dealerships are OUT OF SCOPE everywhere** (operator directive, settled 2026-08-22). No Hyundai or dual-branded store is a competitor, a benchmark, or a gap to fill — for any rooftop, in any market. None is researched, listed, or referenced by name here.

**Internal organic benchmark — Greenway's own best performers (siblings), NOT competitors.** To keep the pillars and the later post plans grounded in formats Greenway has already proven to work, the planning feeds off the **non-pilot sibling rooftops Greenway Kia North and Greenway Kia West (Orlando)** — both confirmed active in the channel inventory with strong Instagram Reels / TikTok. Their formats (especially **repurposable Reel/TikTok formats**) are treated as **repurposing sources** for the precise content plans/outlines built later — never as competitors, and never surfaced to any Greenway GM as a rival. Sibling metrics (views/followers/engagement) are **TO VERIFY via Sprout** — no sibling number is fabricated or estimated here (OPERATING_RULES §3.7; sibling rooftop-to-address attribution stays TO VERIFY per §3.8). Including these siblings as repurposing sources does **not** change the five pillars or the house-default mix; it expands the format library the specific per-store content plans draw from. The observed success-model formats listed in §1 (Models A–E) already include West/North-based observations (orlando local/sports, Spanish-language, depth reviews); the sibling IG/TikTok formats will be quantified only when Sprout returns their real metrics.

## 1. The five content pillars *(rev-1 detail — superseded for strategy/mix by REVISION 2 above; keep for market/evidence context)*

Each pillar gives: its goal, the audience it serves, the evidence base (success model + competitor finding, both cited), the primary execution channels (FB/IG/TikTok per §6a), example post types, and the per-store application note.

### Pillar 1 — Inventory: "The Real Cars on Our Lot"

- **What it is.** The vehicles actually on the lot: new arrivals, per-vehicle walkarounds, model depth reviews, comparisons, and honest offers tied to a real model/trim. Proves the lot is real and gives every car a discoverable asset.
- **Goal.** Drive leads and dealership visits. Inventory is the demand engine; it answers the shopper's first question ("what do you have, is it real, is it worth a visit").
- **Audience.** In-market shoppers for a new or used vehicle in the store's local market; the largest commercially valuable audience a dealership reaches.
- **Evidence base (both sources).**
  - **Success models:** Model B (per-vehicle inventory walkaround assembly line, OBSERVED on Greenway Ford and Longo Toyota), Model A (offer and incentive promo, OBSERVED on Greenway Kia West/Greenway Kia East/Galpin), Model C (model review and walkaround depth, OBSERVED on Greenway Kia North/West/East) — `CONTENT_MODELS.md` §A–C. Model C specifically earns the highest per-video views of any observed format (North's depth tests at 3.3K–6.1K views vs single/tens on offer Shorts, `SUCCESS_ACCOUNTS_RESEARCH.md` §1.2).
  - **Competitor finding:** on YouTube, Store 05's competitors (Rob Sight's inventory line 8 years old, Blue Springs 13 years old, Metro an archive) proved the per-vehicle walkthrough format **and abandoned it**; no KC competitor runs a current inventory or review line (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §1). Stores 03/04 named competitors similarly lack a current inventory line (Wyatt Johnson near-dormant; Murfreesboro's line is part of a 5–8-year-old library) (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §3). Competitors push "best price"/"trust" as claims with no current content behind them (Metro `Best Price Dealer`, `COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §1.4). **Store 02 (Jacksonville) nuance:** no Jacksonville competitor runs a current inventory or review line either, but **Kia on Atlantic runs a current, consistent offer/promo line** — so Store 02's inventory wedge is depth/utility (real stock + reviews), not promo volume (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §2; `COMPETITIVE_MOVE_PLAN.md` §2).
- **The competitive wedge it answers.** Own the current, consistent, real inventory video lane. Competitors once ran it and stopped; running it now with real stock is the CONFIRMED opening. Lead with proof (real cars) over price claim.
- **Primary channels (per §6a).** Facebook (inventory photo post, "this week on the lot"), Instagram (inventory Reel + carousel), TikTok (per-vehicle short, offer short). YouTube (per-vehicle Short weekly + one long-form review monthly) and GBP (new-arrival post) are supporting surfaces.
- **Example post types.** New-arrival walkaround (60s vertical). Per-vehicle short coded by stock/VIN. Trim review clip cut from a long-form. Model comparison ("F-150 vs the competition"). Honest offer short on a real model, real path. "This week on the lot" roundup photo. Sold/thanks post.
- **Per-store application note.** Inventory leads at every rooftop, but the specifics differ: Kia stores (01–04) prioritize the models each market buys (Telluride, Sportage, Sorento, Carnival, EV for Kia) and new-arrival plus used-lot walkarounds; Store 05 (Ford) prioritizes trucks (F-150, Bronco, Maverick, Super Duty) and a multi-brand used-lot line, given a pre-owned-heavy market. Store 05's buyers skew older, so Facebook and Google surfaces carry more weight there (`GM_KPI_FRAMEWORK.md` §2.5). **Every vehicle and offer is TO VERIFY via the real inventory feed; never post a car not verifiably on the lot** (`CONTENT_STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md` §3).

### Pillar 2 — Service & Trust: "The People Who Take Care of Your Car"

- **What it is.** The parts/service bays, mobile service, finance, what a service visit includes, maintenance/recall reminders, and real testimonials. Builds trust and drives service appointments and reviews.
- **Goal.** Drive service appointments and build the trust that a shopper buys from. Service is high-value revenue and the trust layer that supports every other pillar.
- **Audience.** Current owners and service customers in the market (repeat business), plus shoppers who judge the dealer by how it treats its existing customers.
- **Evidence base (both sources).**
  - **Success models:** Model D (department, service and trust, OBSERVED on Greenway Ford: "Mobile Service at Greenway Ford!", "Parts Department", "Finance Department", "Customer Testimonial Video Highlights"; and Longo Toyota: "Online Parts Pick Up Made Easy!") — `CONTENT_MODELS.md` §D. Model C also contributes a service-adjacent explainer format (maintenance/feature explainers).
  - **Competitor finding:** at Store 05's market, Bob Sight owns the trust/mobile-service message but posts it low-volume and thin (a mobile-service-van slot ~2 years old) (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §1.1). At Stores 03/04's market, Murfreesboro's service-convenience slots (pickup and delivery, car-buying-from-home) are 5–8 years old and part of the stale "Kia Guy" library; they are a proven local format we run current (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §2.1).
- **The competitive wedge it answers.** Counter the competitor's claimed trust/thin service message with a **current, consistent** service and trust line backed by real department proof. Where a competitor proved a convenience format (mobile service, pickup and delivery) and stopped, run it current.
- **Primary channels (per §6a).** Facebook (service reminders and department features), Instagram (service-bay Reel/Story), TikTok (quick service-tip clips). Google Business Profile (the proof surface: service posts, 100% review and Q&A response within 2 business days, photo refreshes) and YouTube long-form explainers as capacity allows.
- **Example post types.** "What we check at a service visit" explainer. Seasonal maintenance tip (oil, tires, recall). Service-bay feature. Mobile-service or pickup-and-delivery convenience slot. Customer testimonial (real, named, two-source verified). Review-response and Q&A work on GBP.
- **Per-store application note.** This is a lead pillar at every rooftop but especially at Store 05 (service-heavy Ford market; run mobile-service convenience current — the format competitors proved and abandoned) and at Stores 03/04 (run Murfreesboro's service-convenience format current, each store its own voice and its own service facts). Kia stores (01–04) add recall/maintenance reminders and "what we check at a service visit." **Frame reputation findings as an absence problem, never a complaint list** (OPERATING_RULES §10). Service facts TO VERIFY per store.

### Pillar 3 — People & Place (Behind-the-Scenes): "Meet the Faces of Our Store"

- **What it is.** The real people and the real place: named staff, delivery day, the detail bay, a day at the dealership. Makes the store a known local business with faces, not an anonymous lot.
- **Goal.** Break trust, humanize the store, and give the market a recognizable voice. This is the personality channel on TikTok and the BTS layer on Instagram/Facebook.
- **Audience.** Local followers who buy people before they buy cars; the audience that makes a dealership feel like a neighbor rather than out-of-town.
- **Evidence base (both sources).**
  - **Success models:** Model E (people, showroom, delivery day — OBSERVED on Greenway Kia West's people/showroom/delivery content, and Galpin's showroom walkthrough and personality) — `CONTENT_MODELS.md` §E, `SUCCESS_ACCOUNTS_RESEARCH.md` §2.2. Greenway Ford's department/people titles also ground the people layer.
  - **Competitor finding:** Murfreesboro's "Kia Guy" built a recognizable person around a Nashville-area Kia store and paired it with service convenience — the **closest local evidence that a personality-led Kia dealership resonates in the Middle-TN market** — but it is 5–8 years old (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §2.1). Galpin shows a supercar-halo and showroom personality model (Observed; the "#1 volume dealer" claim is the account's own and unverified) (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §2.2 / `SUCCESS_ACCOUNTS_RESEARCH.md` §2.2).
- **The competitive wedge it answers.** Own the current people/personality lane. Murfreesboro proved the format and went stale; Store 03 and Store 04 each run a current people line with their own real people and voices. Do not clone "The Kia Guy" — each rooftop owns its own recognizable voice.
- **Primary channels (per §6a).** TikTok (the personality home: BTS, staff intro, delivery-day, a day at the dealership), Instagram (Stories for timely/moment content + BTS Reels), Facebook (BTS photo posts). YouTube Short as supporting.
- **Example post types.** Staff intro/spotlight. Delivery-day story. Detail-bay or workshop video. "A day at the dealership" short. Morning-at-the-lot. A walk around the showroom.
- **Per-store application note.** This is the LEAD pillar for Stores 03/04 (the Nashville personality wedge), run separately per rooftop with that rooftop's real people and voice (never one group template). It is a strong secondary layer at all stores. **Named staff in any post require two independent sources before the name enters client-facing copy** (OPERATING_RULES §3.5); people content at every store is TO VERIFY until staff names are confirmed.

### Pillar 4 — Community / Local: "A Local Neighbor in [Market]"

- **What it is.** The store tied to its actual market: real local partnerships, sports ties, events, schools, charities, local moments, and native-language content where the market warrants it.
- **Goal.** Establish local-market standing and differentiate the store from a national ad. Local relevance is what separates a dealership from a corporate billboard.
- **Audience.** The local community; neighbors and local shoppers who value a store that is part of their place.
- **Evidence base (both sources).**
  - **Success models:** Model E (local/community and people — OBSERVED on Greenway Kia West: an Orlando Magic "Drive & Score" partnership hook and Spanish-language Shorts among the highest-viewed on the page; Galpin showroom/halo) — `CONTENT_MODELS.md` §E, `SUCCESS_ACCOUNTS_RESEARCH.md` §1.1, §2.2.
  - **Competitor finding:** competitors show little **current** local-community content on the observed surface (the thin Bob Sight "100+ Years" Heritage/post and Murfreesboro's old holiday slots). A real, current local tie plus delivery-day/people content differentiates the store as a neighbor, not just a lot (`COMPETITIVE_MOVE_PLAN.md` §1 move 5, §2 move 5).
- **The competitive wedge it answers.** Own the local-neighbor lane. Competitors are thin or stale on current community content; a real local tie, run current and local, positions the store as part of its market.
- **Primary channels (per §6a).** Facebook (the local hub: events, sponsorships, community posts), Instagram (Stories for timely local moments), TikTok (local/BTS short). GBP brief local posts as supporting.
- **Example post types.** Local event or sponsorship. Team member in the community. School/club/charity tie. Local landmark, weather, or seasonal moment. Native-language content where the market genuinely has a large non-English audience. Delivery-day / local thank-you.
- **Per-store application note.** The most market-specific pillar: West Palm Beach, Jacksonville, Madison, Antioch, and Raytown each have different local hooks, sports ties, and demographics (**TO VERIFY per store via Shannon/GMs — never assume**). Native-language content only where the market really warrants it (TO VERIFY per market; the observed Spanish-language success was in Orlando, a different market). Local hooks are TO VERIFY before any post.

### Pillar 5 — Reviews & Proof: "What Our Customers Say"

- **What it is.** Real customer proof: reviews, ratings, delivery stories, milestones. Converts by showing that other people trusted the store.
- **Goal.** Convert and feed Google review velocity. Proof is the trust currency a shopper weighs before they visit.
- **Audience.** Near-decision shoppers who check reviews before choosing a dealer (97% read reviews for local businesses; 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews; 83% asked to review will do it — `BENCHMARKS.md` §3.2 via `CONTENT_STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md` §6.6).
- **Evidence base (both sources).**
  - **Success models:** Model D's testimonials layer (OBSERVED on Greenway Ford's "Customer Testimonial Video Highlights" and "Excellence is the Standard!") — `CONTENT_MODELS.md` §D. Model E's delivery-day/people layer feeds proof stories. The review-velocity targets are evidence-based (`BENCHMARKS.md` §3.2 — 20+ recent reviews at 4.0★+, monthly new reviews, 100% genuine response within 2 business days).
  - **Competitor finding:** competitors lead with trust/price **claims** (Bob Sight "Trust Is Our Tradition", Metro "Best Price Dealer") with no current proof behind them (`COMPETITOR_RESEARCH.md` §1.1, §1.4). Observed team finding (not a competitor observation): named individual staff drive nearly all positive reviews at every rooftop — a finding to handle in GM one-on-ones, not as a complaint list (OPERATING_RULES §11).
- **The competitive wedge it answers.** Win on demonstrated proof where competitors only claim. Real, current reviews and delivery stories convert where a standing "trust" slogan does not.
- **Primary channels (per §6a).** Facebook (review shout-outs, delivery stories), Instagram (delivery Reel, review graphic), TikTok (quick testimonial clip). Google Business Profile is the primary proof surface (100% review and Q&A response within 2 business days; profile completeness; 20+ recent photos) — `CONTENT_STRATEGY_FRAMEWORK.md` §6.6.
- **Example post types.** Customer review graphic. Delivery story. Review milestone ("X reviews — thank you"). Before/after service result. Testimonial video (real, named, two-source verified).
- **Per-store application note.** A lead pillar for any store working on reputation or review velocity (all of them, per GM_KPI_FRAMEWORK, with Stores 01/03/04 rated 4.3–4.5★ and review counts TO VERIFY). Review responses are calendar rows, not afterthoughts (CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE §3). **Never incentivize reviews (prohibited by Google), never copy-paste replies, never fabricate a review or rating.** Sensitive review patterns stay in GM one-on-ones (OPERATING_RULES §9).
