## 0. How to use this document

This is the methodology the team uses to build **one store's content strategy at a time** — never the group as a whole. It produces three working artifacts per store, in order:

1. **A per-store strategy** (`content/strategies/store-XX/STRATEGY.md` — filled from this framework)
2. **A per-store, per-month calendar** (`content/calendars/store-XX/MONTH-01.md` … `MONTH-06.md` — filled from `CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE.md`)
3. **A creative brief per piece of content** (`content/briefs/store-XX/<content-id>.md` — filled from `CREATIVE_BRIEF.md`)

**No store facts exist yet.** Until Shannon McNeil provides the data listed in `CLIENT.md` §8, every store-specific field in a strategy is marked:

> **TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil**

This framework is the machine that runs once that data arrives. Nothing here describes Store 01–05 specifically; it describes how any one of them gets its strategy built.

## 3. Content pillars — what a store posts about

Five pillars cover everything a dealership can post. They are the same five for every store — **what changes per store is which pillars dominate, based on its audit, its market, and its goals.**

| Pillar | What it is | Example slots (fill per store — TO VERIFY topics) |
|---|---|---|
| **Inventory** | The cars actually on the lot: new arrivals, spotlights, walkarounds, comparisons, price-feature posts | New-arrival walkaround · inventory spotlight (photo/video) · model comparison · "this week on the lot" roundup · sold/thanks post |
| **Service** | What the service department does: maintenance tips, reminders, appointments, shop stories | Seasonal maintenance tip · service reminder (e.g., oil, tires, recall) · service-bay feature · "what we check at a service visit" explainer |
| **Community / Local** | The store as a local neighbor: events, sponsorships, school/club/charity ties, local moments | Local event or sponsorship · team member in the community · local landmark/weather/seasonal post · charity drive |
| **Behind-the-scenes** | The people and the place: staff, detail bay, delivery day, morning at the dealership | Staff intro/spotlight · delivery-day story · detail bay or workshop video · "a day at the dealership" |
| **Reviews & Proof** | Real customer proof: reviews, ratings, delivery stories, milestones | Customer review graphic · delivery story · review milestone (e.g., "X reviews — thank you") · before/after service result |

**Per-store pillar selection (do this in `STRATEGY.md`):**
1. From the audit: which content topics already get the most engagement for this store? (evidence, or TO VERIFY)
2. From the store's goals: does this store need leads (→ lean Inventory), service appointments (→ lean Service), or reputation repair (→ lean Reviews & Proof)? (goals TO VERIFY)
3. From the market: what local hooks exist? (TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil)
4. Write the pillar set as **percentages** (see §7 mix ratios), not as a menu of everything.

**Rule:** never post inventory that is not verifiably on the store's lot. Every inventory slot's vehicle details are **TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil** until the store's inventory feed/listing is provided.

## 4. Voice & tone — how the store sounds

Dealerships earn trust by sounding like **a knowledgeable local neighbor, not a radio ad**. One consistent voice across all 6 channels, adapted per channel (see §6), and reviewed by Shannon before it is used anywhere.

| Principle | Plain-language meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Honest first** | Never claim what isn't true: no fake urgency, no "one day only" unless real, no photos of cars that aren't there | "This week on the lot" with real photos, not stock shots |
| **Specific beats vague** | Real model, real trim, real price path, real location | "2022 X in the showroom" over "great deals!" |
| **Helpful beats pushy** | Answer the shopper's question before asking for the sale | "When to swap winter tires in [market]" over "BUY NOW" |
| **Human, not corporate** | Written by a person, signed by a person where it fits | Staff intro posts with real names (names TO VERIFY) |
| **Local first** | Reference the store's actual market, weather, events | Community pillar slots (TO VERIFY local hooks) |

**Tone guardrails (apply to every piece of content):**
- Plain language a GM and a shopper both understand; no agency jargon, no hype words ("unbelievable," "act fast," "only 2 left" — the last one is only ever allowed when the deadline/limit is real).
- No invented facts, prices, inventory, or performance claims. Anything unverified carries **TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil** internally and is never published that way.
- Follow the client's OEM/compliance rules once provided (TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil).
- Reddit has its own voice rules — see §6.5. That channel is participation, not broadcast.

## 6. Channel-by-channel strategy

### 6.1 Facebook

- **Role:** the store's local hub — where the market's shoppers find hours, phone, events, and proof. Treat messages as a sales channel: 55% of consumers use Facebook to contact brands (`BENCHMARKS.md` §3.1), and pages that respond to ≥90% of messages can earn Meta's "Very responsive" badge (`BENCHMARKS.md` §3.1).
- **Formats:** photo posts are the standout format — e.g., Rival IQ 2025 measured photo posts driving roughly 5× the median engagement rate for top-form platforms such as Sports Teams (`BENCHMARKS.md` §1.3). Lean into real local photos: lot, showroom, service bays, staff, events.
- **Also use:** local events (create actual event pages), service reminders, offers, and review shout-outs.
- **Cadence:** 3–5 posts/week. The all-industry average is ~5/day, which Sprout Social itself calls "a high bar"; for a single dealership, consistency beats volume (`BENCHMARKS.md` §2). No multi-week silences.
- **Community management:** reply to every comment; answer messages same business day at minimum.
- **Avoid:** link-only posts, stock-photo filler, and anything that reads as an ad without local value.

### 6.2 Instagram

- **Role:** visual inventory + lifestyle for the store's market; the channel where Reels and Stories earn the most surface.
- **Formats:** Reels (short video: walkarounds, delivery-day, staff, local moments), carousel photo posts for inventory spotlights, and Stories for timely content (new arrival, event day, polls, link taps). Track saves — saves signal intent (`AUDIT_FRAMEWORK.md` §4.2).
- **Cadence:** 3–5 feed posts/week plus 3–5 Stories/week. Benchmarks: all-industry average ≈1–2 posts/day; the audit floor is 3–5×/week with no multi-week gaps (`BENCHMARKS.md` §2). Final count depends on Shannon-confirmed capacity (TO VERIFY).
- **Profile:** bio says what the store is + location; one clean link (link-in-bio); highlights organized by pillar.
- **Community management:** reply to comments; answer DMs same business day (Meta response expectations, `BENCHMARKS.md` §3.1).
- **Avoid:** hashtag spam, reposted generic memes, and captions with no local relevance.

### 6.3 TikTok

- **Role:** discovery and personality — the channel where the store shows it is a real place with real people.
- **Formats:** short, authentic vertical video with sound-on captions and on-screen text; trends and sounds only when they fit a dealership's tone (never forced). BTS, delivery day, detail bay, quick tips.
- **Cadence:** 3–5 videos/week to start. Platform guidance is 1–4 posts/day (`BENCHMARKS.md` §2); a dealership that is one of five stores in a pilot will rarely sustain that, so the house default is the lower end — **final cadence is a Shannon-approved capacity decision** (TO VERIFY). Consistency matters more than volume.
- **Community management:** reply to comments within 48 hours (house rule, `BENCHMARKS.md` §3.3).
- **Avoid:** overproduced ads, soundless videos without captions, and jumping on trends that mock or misrepresent the store.

### 6.4 YouTube

- **Role:** depth — the channel where a shopper can learn before they visit. Shorts for discovery, long-form for value.
- **Formats:** **Shorts** (vertical, ≤60s) for new arrivals, quick service tips, walkarounds; **long-form** (detailed walkarounds, comparisons, service explainers, customer stories) monthly to start. Every video gets a real thumbnail and a descriptive title with local keywords; videos are grouped into playlists (e.g., "New Arrivals," "Service Tips") (`BENCHMARKS.md` §6).
- **Cadence:** 1 Short/week + 1 long-form/month to start; **no unexplained gap longer than 2–3 weeks** (house rule from `BENCHMARKS.md` §6; YouTube publishes no official frequency).
- **Community management:** reply to comments within 48 hours (house rule, `BENCHMARKS.md` §3.3).
- **Avoid:** clickbait thumbnails, hour-long unedited footage, and uploads with no description or keyword title.

### 6.5 Reddit

- **Role:** community participation — **not a broadcast channel.** Reddit's Reddiquette is explicit: it's fine to be a redditor with a website; it's not okay to be a website with a redditor account. Far more participating than promoting (`BENCHMARKS.md` §5).
- **What works:** genuine answers to local or car-related questions (e.g., in fitting local/car subreddits), helpful, non-promotional comments; a mention of the store is earned by usefulness, never pushed.
- **Gate before any Reddit activity:** the audit must confirm **subreddit fit** — which subreddits exist for the store's market and what their rules say (`BENCHMARKS.md` §5). If no fit exists, the strategy says so and Reddit is skipped or deferred — a deliberate decision, not an assumption.
- **Cadence:** no posting-frequency benchmark exists (`BENCHMARKS.md` §7). House default: 2–4 genuine participations/week (comments/answers), only after subreddit fit is confirmed.
- **Account posture:** participate as a person (u/), not a billboard. No offers, no links to the store's pages in a promotional voice. Reply to replies within 24 hours where notified (house rule, `BENCHMARKS.md` §3.3).
- **Avoid:** hard-selling in any form; a dealership account that posts offers in local/car subreddits will be removed and can hurt the store's reputation (`BENCHMARKS.md` §5).

### 6.6 Google Business Profile

- **Role:** local search and proof — often the first thing a shopper sees before they ever visit socials.
- **What works (with evidence from `BENCHMARKS.md` §3.2 and §4.1):**
  - **Reviews:** 97% of consumers read online reviews for local businesses; 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews; 31% will only use a business rated 4.5★ or higher; 74% only consider reviews from the last 3 months. Target: **20+ recent reviews at 4.0★+, with new reviews flowing monthly** (ask at delivery and service — 83% of consumers asked to leave a review went on to leave one). Directional target, not a guarantee.
  - **Responses:** 89% of consumers expect the business to respond to their review; 80% are more likely to use a business that responds to all reviews; templated/generic replies put off 50%. House target: **respond to 100% of new reviews within 2 business days, genuinely, not by template**; answer every profile Q&A within 2 business days.
  - **Profile completeness:** current logo, cover, 20+ recent photos (lot, showroom, service), accurate hours, correct category, and NAP (name/address/phone) identical everywhere (`BENCHMARKS.md` §4.2–4.3).
  - **Posts:** Google publishes no posting-frequency number (`BENCHMARKS.md` §7); house default is 2–4 brief posts/month (new arrival, service offer, community item) plus photo refreshes.
- **Cadence:** 2–4 posts/month + continuous review/Q&A response + monthly photo additions.
- **Avoid:** incentivized reviews (prohibited by Google, `BENCHMARKS.md` §3.2), fake Q&A, and copy-paste review replies.

## 7. Content-mix ratios (house defaults)

These are the team's **starting defaults**, to be set per store in `STRATEGY.md` based on audit evidence, store goals, and market — never treated as fixed. They are house rules, not sourced benchmark claims (`BENCHMARKS.md` has no automotive content-mix data; see §9).

### 7.1 Overall monthly mix (per store, all channels combined)

| Pillar | Default share of posts | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory | 30% | Leads come from cars; photo/video formats prove the lot is real |
| Service | 20% | Service appointments are high-value; tips earn trust |
| Community / Local | 20% | Local relevance is what separates a dealership from a national ad |
| Behind-the-scenes | 15% | People and place build the "real local business" feel |
| Reviews & Proof | 15% | Proof converts; also feeds GBP review velocity |

### 7.2 Per-channel emphasis (apply the overall mix through each channel's lens)

| Channel | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Facebook | Balance all five; lean Community/Local and Service reminders; photos preferred |
| Instagram | Lean Inventory (Reels + carousels) and BTS; Community/Local in Stories |
| TikTok | Lean BTS and Inventory; authentic video; Service tips as quick clips |
| YouTube | Shorts = Inventory + BTS discovery; long-form = Service/explainers and Inventory depth |
| Reddit | Community/Local participation only — no pillar quotas; participate, don't promote |
| Google Business Profile | Reviews & Proof (responses, review velocity) + Inventory/Service refreshes + photos |

### 7.3 Monthly mix check

At the end of every month, count the store's actual posts by pillar and by channel and compare to the targets in `STRATEGY.md`. Record the check in the month's calendar (`CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE.md` §6). If a pillar is over- or under-served by more than ~10 percentage points for two months running, rebalance the next month's calendar — the mix is a plan, not a straitjacket, but drift must be noticed, not accidental.

## 8. Posting cadence per channel (aligned with `audit/BENCHMARKS.md`)

House-default starting cadence **per store**. These are starting points to be finalized when Shannon confirms capacity and each store's goals (TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil). Consistency beats volume on every channel.

| Channel | Pilot starting cadence (per store) | Benchmark basis (BENCHMARKS.md) |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook | 3–5 posts/week | §2 — industry avg ~5/day is a high bar; consistency beats volume |
| Instagram | 3–5 feed posts/week + 3–5 Stories/week | §2 — avg ≈1–2 posts/day; audit floor 3–5×/week, no multi-week gaps |
| TikTok | 3–5 videos/week | §2 — platform guidance 1–4/day; dealership-appropriate lower bound |
| YouTube | 1 Short/week + 1 long-form/month | §6 — no official frequency; no gap > 2–3 weeks |
| Reddit | 2–4 genuine participations/week (after subreddit fit confirmed) | §5, §7 — no frequency benchmark exists; participation norm |
| Google Business Profile | 2–4 posts/month + 100% review & Q&A response within 2 business days + monthly photo refresh | §3.2, §4, §7 — no official frequency; response targets are evidence-based |

**Rough monthly output per store:** ~20–28 scheduled posts (FB + IG + TikTok + YouTube + GBP) plus ~8–16 Reddit participations plus review/Q&A responses. This is a **capacity question for Shannon**, not a target the team sets alone — confirm before locking any store's calendar (TO VERIFY).

**Context for expectations:** engagement rates fell year-over-year on every major platform (Facebook −36%, Instagram −16%, TikTok −34% — Rival IQ 2025, `BENCHMARKS.md` §1.1). Declining engagement is a platform-wide trend, not a store failure; the store's trend is compared to the trend, and to its own history and goals — never to a pass/fail line. No automotive-specific engagement medians are published in verifiable form; Retail is the closest labeled proxy (`BENCHMARKS.md` §1.2, §7).

## 10. Per-store process — end to end

For **each store** (Store 01–05), in order:

1. **Data gate (via Shannon).** Confirm the store's identity, handles, account status/ownership, goals, assets, and compliance rules (`CLIENT.md` §8; `channel-matrix.csv`; `stores/store-XX/STORE.md`). Nothing real happens before this.
2. **Audit.** Run the per-store, per-channel audit (`audit/AUDIT_FRAMEWORK.md`) → `audit/reports/store-XX/AUDIT_REPORT.md` (internal draft → team lead → Shannon → client).
3. **Strategy.** From audit findings + goals + market, write `content/strategies/store-XX/STRATEGY.md` using this framework: pillars + mix (%), voice notes, per-channel plan, cadence, proof points, and the store's TO VERIFY list.
4. **Calendar.** Build Month 01–06 from `CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE.md` → `content/calendars/store-XX/MONTH-01.md` … `MONTH-06.md`. One month at a time, aligned to the mix and cadence in the strategy.
5. **Creative briefs.** For each calendar slot, fill `CREATIVE_BRIEF.md` → `content/briefs/store-XX/<content-id>.md` before copy is written. Brief first, copy second.
6. **Copy, review, approve, post.** Draft copy per brief → team lead review → **Shannon McNeil approval** → only Shannon posts or sends to the store to post (`WORKFLOW.md`). Update copy/approval/posted statuses in the calendar as each item moves.
7. **Monthly mix check + retro.** Count actual posts vs. mix targets; note what performed; feed the findings into next month's calendar and the next audit round (`CONTENT_CALENDAR_TEMPLATE.md` §6).
