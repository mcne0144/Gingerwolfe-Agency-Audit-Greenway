## 2. Why it worked

1. The stakes are real, specific, and denominated in dollars. "$5000 bonus" plus "15k followers" gives the audience a scoreboard and a job: following the account literally advances the plot.
2. "chat" in the opening text casts viewers as participants in a livestream-style story, which is why comments (934) massively outrun shares (339). This format farms conversation, not reach.
3. Serialization: the episode ends unresolved, so the only way to learn the outcome is to follow. This is the account's follower engine, distinct from the reach engine (BRIEF 03).
4. The managers play reluctant gatekeepers, which gives the character a recurring antagonist without villainizing anyone; the whole store is visibly in on it.
5. It cost nothing: one phone, one hallway, one bullpen, no set dressing, one meme edit.

## 3. Reshootable template

1. Prerequisite: an established named character the audience already knows (do not launch a serialized arc as episode one of a character; West ran Zarya content before this arc).
2. Pick a real, store-confirmed goal with a number in it: a follower threshold tied to a real bonus or store commitment, a sales milestone, an event target. The number must be true; the no-fabrication rule applies to stakes exactly as to offers.
3. Structure per episode, 40–60s: (a) 0–3s follow shot into the location with stakes text on screen from frame one, phrased to the audience ("Will she get it chat?"); (b) 20–35s of pleading/negotiating with the gatekeeper (manager plays themselves, light direction only); (c) optional one meme insert as a comedy beat; (d) final 8–10s direct-to-camera plea with the explicit ask text ("Help X get to Y so Z happens"); (e) end unresolved.
4. Cadence: one episode every 1–2 weeks per §7, each advancing the goal (progress update, setback, near-miss), with the resolution episode saved for when the goal is actually hit.
5. Captions: the plea, verbatim pattern "Help [name] get the [stake]😭🙏". Word captions on dialogue if publishing beyond TikTok.
6. Compliance: the gatekeeper is in on it and approves their portrayal; the bonus/goal terms are real and cleared by the GM before episode one; no customer-facing promises are made in-story.
