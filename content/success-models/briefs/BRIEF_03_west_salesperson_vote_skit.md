## 1. Correction to the analysis doc

§2.2 and §7 describe this as a "two-character skit (pushy vs honest)". The frames show it is actually a **multi-candidate vote format**: Zarya acts as host inside the showroom and introduces at least four different salespeople by name, and each one exits the showroom's front door to greet an off-screen customer in his own style. The correction matters for reshoots: the format's engine is the repeated setup-payoff loop plus audience voting, not a single A/B contrast. §7 adaptations built on a two-person version remain valid as a simplified variant, but the full format is stronger.

## 3. Why it worked

1. The caption is a ballot. "Who would you buy a car from?" turns watching into voting, which produced 2,693 comments and an absurd 44.6K shares (people tag the friend who behaves like candidate 2). The format manufactures its own comment section.
2. The repeated structure (intro inside, exit outside, next candidate) creates a predictable loop with a fresh punchline every 12–15 seconds. Predictability holds retention across 67s; a skit this long otherwise dies (the analysis doc's 2-minute talking head did 804 plays).
3. The same locked doorway shot makes every personality difference funny by contrast. It is a fair test, and audiences treat fair tests as content.
4. It humanizes the sales team without a single sales message. No car, no offer, no price appears in the video.
5. Zarya as host links it to the account's running character universe, so skit viewers convert to followers who then see serialized content (BRIEF 04).

## 4. Reshootable template

1. Cast: one host (the store's named character where one exists) and three to four real salespeople with visibly different energy. Same uniform for all candidates.
2. Two setups only: (a) interior host spot with decent light and the store visible behind; (b) locked tripod shot of the main entrance from outside, wide enough to show the full walk.
3. Structure per candidate, 12–15s: host introduces the candidate by first name and dispatches them to a waiting customer, cut to the locked door shot, candidate exits and walks it their way. Three to four candidates, total 50–70s.
4. Direction to candidates: one word each (smooth, eager, boss, bouncy). Let real personality do the rest; do not script the walks.
5. Word-by-word center captions on all interior dialogue for sound-off viewing.
6. Caption: the ballot question, verbatim pattern "Who would you buy a car from?😅😎" plus store hashtags. Never answer the question in the caption or comments.
7. Compliance: first names only on screen unless the two-source rule for named staff is met for client-facing reuse. No customer appears on camera (the customer stays off-screen, which also avoids any release issue).
