## 1. What actually happens (from frames)

The whole video is one joke, no dialogue, no on-screen text.

## 2. Why it worked

1. The absurd image is fully legible in the first second. There is no setup to sit through; the thumbnail itself is the joke.
2. Six seconds means near-guaranteed completion and instant loops, which TikTok's distribution rewards. This is the mechanical reason a mid-size dealership account hit 7.0M.
3. The caption carries the second beat of the joke ("I'd be scared too" reframes the parked rival SUVs as frightened onlookers). Viewers who check the caption get a payoff, and caption-checking is an engagement signal.
4. Personification of the product (car as giant pet) is a repeatable comedy engine that needs zero acting skill and zero dialogue.
5. 16.4K saves and 3K shares on a humor clip: people send it to friends. Share-bait humor, not sales content, and the account's follower base was built by exactly this (417 followers before the June Telluride clips, 34.6K after; analysis doc §1).

## 3. Reshootable template

1. Cast: one staff member comfortable on camera (no lines to deliver), one driver hidden low in the vehicle, one phone operator.
2. Rig: 15–20 ft of light rope or dog leash, taped or looped to a tow-hook area low on the front of the SUV. Nothing that can scratch; test the tape first.
3. Location: any open lane on the store's own lot. Do not clear the background; parked inventory facing the lane is part of the joke.
4. Shot list, two setups: (a) locked wide, subject walks toward camera with the vehicle creeping alongside at idle speed, 3–4s; (b) locked side profile, same action across frame, 3–4s. Cut them together, total 5–7s. No text overlay, no music-dependent gag (works muted).
5. Caption pattern: one deadpan line that voices a bystander or the rival cars, plus model and store hashtags. Do not explain the joke.
6. Safety and compliance: walking-pace idle only, spotter present, private lot, no public road. The vehicle shown must be on that store's lot; use the store's actual best-selling SUV.
7. Pin it if it outperforms, per the pin-refresh rule (analysis doc §5).
