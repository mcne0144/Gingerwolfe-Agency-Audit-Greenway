## 1. Executive summary

Greenway does not need to look outside its own group to see what works in dealership social. Three of its Orlando Kia stores (North, West, East) already run the content model this pilot proposes, and their numbers prove it at every level:

1. **Format decides results, not budget or following.** The group's best organic posts are short feature demos with question hooks (up to 7.0 million plays on a 6-second clip), a named recurring on-camera character (1.2 million plays, 44,600 shares), and checklist-style "why own this model" videos. The same stores' offer graphics, static posts, and delivery-day carousels earn near zero, every time.
2. **Offers work as videos with people, not as graphics.** The cleanest test came from one store posting the same kind of lease offer both ways: as a plain offer graphic it earned 227 to 436 plays; as a character-led video with the full disclaimer in the caption it earned 118,200 plays and 1,502 saves. This is now a standing rule for the pilot: an offer ships as a video with a person, or it does not ship.
3. **The pilot stores' current reach is almost entirely paid, and their organic baseline is close to zero.** Owned analytics show 96 to 99 percent of August Facebook impressions at the connected pilot stores came from paid delivery, with single-digit to low-double-digit engagement per store for the month. The honest organic starting line is now measured per store (section 4), and all pilot reporting will state organic and paid separately so progress is never miscounted.
4. **The operating pattern the pilot proposes is already proven inside Greenway.** One production pipeline visibly feeds all three Orlando stores with localized posts. The pilot's hub-and-spoke model asks the group to extend a pattern it already runs, not to adopt an agency import.

The five best sibling posts have been converted into shot-by-shot, reshootable creative briefs mapped to each pilot rooftop's staffing reality (section 5), so execution can begin from proven formats on day one.

## 2. What we studied and how

**Internal benchmark (public data).** Full retrieval of recent Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok activity for Greenway Kia North, Greenway Kia West, and Greenway Kia East (Orlando market): 185 posts and profile statistics across the three stores, retrieved 2026-08-29. These stores are studied as internal benchmarks and repurposing sources. They are never framed as rivals to any pilot store.

**Owned analytics (Sprout Social, read-only).** Connection inventory of the group's Sprout account (46 connected profiles) and daily August analytics for the five connected pilot profiles and six sibling profiles, including the organic versus paid impression split. No publishing action was taken; the pull was read-only.

**Cross-validation.** Where a post appears in both datasets, owned metrics matched the public numbers almost exactly (often to the digit). One material mismatch surfaced: public Facebook video view counters can include paid delivery, so they are not treated as organic evidence anywhere in this report.

**Video review.** The five top-performing sibling posts were watched frame by frame and documented as reshootable briefs. Audio was not transcribed; the briefs quote no dialogue and claim only what frames and platform metadata show.

## 3. The internal benchmark: what works at Greenway's own stores

Ranked by observed ceiling, with the evidence:

| # | Format | Best observed result | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short feature demo with a question hook (6 to 46s) | 7.0M, 730K, 218K plays (Kia North) | One phone, one car, one feature. No acting. |
| 2 | Named recurring character with a storyline | 1.2M plays, 2,693 comments (Kia West) | A willing on-camera person and continuity |
| 3 | Offer as character-led video, disclaimer in caption | 118.2K plays, 1,502 saves (Kia West) | A real store-confirmed offer and a presenter |
| 4 | New-model launch walkaround | 13.5K plays on a 571-follower account (Kia West) | The vehicle on the lot, 60 to 90 seconds |
| 5 | Checklist "why own this model" video | 4.2K Instagram and the best observed Facebook result (Kia East) | Text overlay and footage only, no talent |
| 6 | Service and ownership tips from a technician | 2.5K to 3.9K plays (Kia West) | A named tech and one seasonal tip |
| 7 | Real team moments (a real new hire, a real name) | 3.7K plays with warm comments (Kia West) | Specificity, not "meet our team" generics |
| 8 | Community sponsorship video | The highest Facebook share count observed (14 shares, Kia North) | A verified local hook; Facebook-first |

**What does not work, on the same accounts:** plain offer graphics and offer-copy videos (227 to 675 plays), static posts and corporate sales-event graphics (0 to 3 reactions), delivery-day congratulation carousels (near zero, no shares), two-minute talking-head trust monologues (804 plays), and letting a channel go dormant while the store's best content runs elsewhere.

**Cadence that works:** the active sibling accounts hold 8 to 12 posts per month per platform (2 to 3 per week), and every one of their all-time best posts is pinned to the profile. Consistency at that rhythm beats both silence and daily bursts in the observed data.

**Engagement reality check for goal-setting:** on healthy dealership video, likes run 2 to 4 percent of plays; comments concentrate almost entirely on character and humor content; shares and saves are the signals that predict distribution. Follower count is a lagging floor, not a ceiling: these stores reach 2,000 to 13,500 plays on accounts with 400 to 600 followers. Pilot stores' small followings are not a limitation and will not be presented to GMs as one.

## 5. Proven formats, ready to reshoot (strategy component)

Each of the five briefs documents one top sibling post shot by shot, explains why it worked mechanically, and provides a numbered reshoot template a store employee with a phone can execute. Formats are copied; footage never is. Every adaptation uses the store's own lot, stock, people, and verified facts.

| Brief | Source result | Which pilot stores run it |
|---|---|---|
| Six-second sight gag (vehicle "walked" on a leash) | 7.0M plays | All; the strongest no-dialogue format for Stores 03/04, which run without an on-camera character |
| Feature demo with question hook (key fob rolls windows down) | 730.4K plays, 4,516 shares | Store 02's weekly series; Stores 03/04; Store 05 as Ford analogs (Pro Power Onboard, tailgate features) |
| Salesperson vote skit ("Who would you buy a car from?") | 1.2M plays, 44.6K shares | Store 01 with its named character; Store 02 once a host is confirmed |
| Serialized character goal episode | 143.9K plays, 934 comments | Store 01; the follower-growth engine once its character is established |
| Offer as character video, full disclaimer in caption | 118.2K plays, 1,502 saves | Every store, as the only approved way to publish an offer |

## 6. What this changes in the strategy (and what it confirms)

1. **The ratified five-pillar content mix survives contact with the data unchanged.** Character content is confirmed as the growth engine, feature demos as the highest-ceiling repeatable unit, and the reduced share for proof/delivery content is validated by its near-zero observed performance.
2. **Three small rules are added to each store's strategy:** question-hook captions on demo content, a 6-to-30-second default length except for skits and walkarounds, and a standing pin-refresh discipline (whenever a post outperforms the current pinned set, the pins change).
3. **Facebook is treated as a mirror and community surface, not a growth surface.** The only formats that moved on Facebook were checklist videos and community content (shares). This matches the ratified Facebook mix.
4. **The governance conversation gets simpler.** Greenway's own Orlando stores are already centrally produced and locally published. The pilot's hub-and-spoke operating model can be presented as extending the group's own proven pattern to five more rooftops, with the train-the-trainer goal of leaving store staff able to run it.
5. **What the pilot will not copy from the siblings:** unverifiable forward-model titles, hype and urgency copy, offer graphics, and channel dormancy. These are documented as explicit exclusions.
