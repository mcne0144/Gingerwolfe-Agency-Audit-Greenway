## 0. How to read this file

- Every benchmark below carries a **real, named source** with a URL.
- **Source type** is labeled for each entry:
  - **Platform-published** = the platform's own documentation (Meta, Google, YouTube, Reddit, TikTok).
  - **Third-party study** = a reputable, named independent research firm (Rival IQ, BrightLocal, Sprout Social).
- **Verification status** is labeled for each entry:
  - **Verified live 2026-08** = the page was fetched and read during preparation of this file.
  - **Canonical doc** = the source is a stable, canonical platform URL; content is well-established but the page could not be re-read on 2026-08 (platform/anti-bot blocks). **Re-verify against the live page before any client-facing use.**
- **Benchmarks are directional, not guarantees.** They describe typical or platform-advised performance. Every store and market differs — the audit uses these numbers as context, never as pass/fail.
- **Nothing here is invented.** Benchmarks that could not be sourced are explicitly listed in §7 rather than approximated.

## 1. Engagement-rate norms (by channel)

Engagement rate = (interactions ÷ impressions or reach) × 100. The audit computes per-post engagement rate from the store's own posts and compares directionally.

### 1.1 All-platform trend
- **Rival IQ 2025 Social Media Industry Benchmark Report** (third-party study; verified live 2026-08) — engagement rates **fell year-over-year on every major platform**: Facebook −36%, Instagram −16%, TikTok −34%, X −48%. Posting frequency rose slightly on TikTok while slowing on Instagram and Facebook.
  - Source: Rival IQ, "2025 Social Media Industry Benchmark Report," https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/
  - **Implication:** declining engagement is a platform-wide trend, not a store failure. Compare the store's trend to the trend, not just to a static number.

### 1.2 Note on industry-specific engagement rates
- The Rival IQ 2025 report publishes **median engagement rates by platform and by industry** (all-industry and 14 named industries). **Automotive is not one of the 14 industry sections in the 2025 edition** (verified live 2026-08: sections are Alcohol, Fashion, Financial Services, Food & Beverage, Health & Beauty, Higher Ed, Home Decor, Influencers, Media, Nonprofits, Retail, Sports Teams, Tech & Software, Travel). **Retail is the closest published proxy for dealerships** and should be labeled as a proxy, not as automotive data.
  - Source: same Rival IQ report URL above.
- **Explicit non-find:** a dealership-specific, source-verified engagement-rate median could not be obtained from a reputable named study at time of writing. **No automotive engagement-rate number is stated here rather than inventing one.** The audit therefore scores engagement against (a) the all-industry medians in the Rival IQ report, (b) the store's own history, and (c) its pilot goals.

### 1.3 Format note (Facebook)
- Rival IQ 2025 (verified live 2026-08): **photo posts** drove standout Facebook engagement (e.g., Sports Teams earned ~5× the median engagement rate on Facebook with photo posts).
  - Source: https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/
  - **Implication:** real, local photos (inventory, service bays, staff) are a high-leverage format for dealership pages.

## 3. Response-time expectations (messages, comments, reviews)

### 3.1 Meta (Facebook / Instagram messaging)
- **Platform-published:** Meta's **responsiveness badge** — pages that respond to **≥90% of messages** with a fast response time can earn a "Very responsive" badge; response time is measured in Business Suite and shown to visitors (the widely documented threshold has been ~90% response rate and ~15-minute average response time).
  - Source: Meta Business Help Center, "What is the responsiveness badge and how do I get one?" https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1610193742333568 (platform-published; canonical doc — Meta blocked live re-read on 2026-08; **re-verify before client use**)
- **Third-party:** 55% of consumers use Facebook to contact brands (Sprout Social Q2 2025 consumer survey, reported on the "How often to post" page — verified live 2026-08; source URL as §2).
  - **Implication:** dealerships should treat Facebook/Instagram messages as a sales channel and answer same-business-day at minimum.

### 3.2 Google Business Profile reviews & Q&A
- **Third-party study (BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026):**
  - **89%** of consumers expect the business to respond to their review; **19%** expect a same-day response, **32%** by the next day, **81%** within a week.
  - **80%** of consumers are more likely to use a business that responds to all reviews; **templated/generic replies put off 50%**.
  - Source: BrightLocal, "Local Consumer Review Survey 2026," https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/ (third-party study; verified live 2026-08; methodology: 1,002 US adult consumers via SurveyMonkey)
- **Platform-published:** Google's Business Profile Help says to **reply to reviews** ("Show customers that their feedback matters") and to **respond in a timely manner** ("A prompt response shows that you value your customers' feedback"); incentivized reviews are prohibited.
  - Source: Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to get more reviews," https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122 (platform-published; verified live 2026-08)
  - **Implication:** target: respond to **100% of new reviews within 2 business days**, with a genuine, non-templated reply; answer every profile Q&A within 2 business days.

### 3.3 YouTube / TikTok / Reddit comments
- **No platform-published response-time thresholds exist** for YouTube, TikTok, or Reddit comments. House rule for the pilot: reply to comments within 48 hours on YouTube and TikTok; on Reddit, reply within 24 hours where the platform notifies (and follow subreddit norms).

## 4. Google Business Profile benchmarks

### 4.1 Reviews — volume and ratings (third-party study)
From **BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026** (verified live 2026-08; URL in §3.2):
- **97%** of consumers read online reviews for local businesses; **41%** say they "always" read reviews.
- **47%** of consumers won't use a business with **fewer than 20 reviews**; only **9%** will use one with 5 or fewer.
- **92%** care about star rating; **31%** will only use a business rated **4.5 stars or higher** (rising consumer expectations year over year).
- **74%** only consider reviews written in the last **3 months** — review recency matters.
- **93%** have made a purchase after reading reviews.
- Consumers write ~**4–6 reviews per year** on average; **83%** of consumers asked to leave a review went on to leave one — asking works.
- Google remains the top site for reading and writing reviews (71% read Google reviews; 45% wrote a Google review in the past year).
- **Implication for dealerships:** target **20+ recent reviews with a 4.0+ average**, keep new reviews flowing monthly (ask at delivery/service), and respond to all of them. These are directional targets, not guarantees of outcomes.

### 4.2 Profile completeness and photos
- **Platform-published:** Google's Business Profile Help instructs businesses to keep their profile complete (name, address, phone, hours, category, photos) so customers "get discovered"; review/photo management is part of the profile workflow.
  - Source: Google Business Profile Help, "Get Listed on Google," https://support.google.com/business/ (platform-published; verified live 2026-08 that the help center's get-started content says a complete profile with essential information helps customers find the business)
- **Explicit non-find:** the frequently quoted Google figures ("profiles with photos get 42% more requests for directions and 35% more website clicks"; "complete profiles are 2.7× more likely to be considered reputable") could **not be re-verified** on a live Google page at time of writing (the original Google blog post returns 404; Wayback Machine was rate-limited). **These numbers are excluded from this file** rather than cited from memory. See §7.
  - **House rule instead:** every dealership profile should have a current logo, cover, 20+ recent photos (lot, showroom, service), accurate hours, and consistent NAP — verified against Google's help center at audit time.

### 4.3 Category and NAP
- **Platform-published:** Google uses the profile's category, name, address, and phone to determine what the business is and where it shows. Keeping category accurate and NAP identical across the web (profile, website, socials, third-party listings) is standard local-search practice.
  - Source: Google Business Profile Help, https://support.google.com/business/ (platform-published; canonical)

## 5. Reddit benchmarks and norms

- **Platform-published (canonical doc):** Reddit's **Reddiquette** states that links to your own content are acceptable **within reason**, but posting your own content constantly is not — the community standard is **far more participating than promoting** ("it's perfectly fine to be a redditor with a website, it's not okay to be a website with a redditor account" is Reddit's long-standing guidance). Subreddits set their own rules and enforce them.
  - Source: Reddit Help, "Reddiquette," https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000672792-Reddiquette (platform-published; canonical doc — Reddit's help center blocked live re-read on 2026-08; **re-verify before client use**)
  - Related: Reddit Content Policy, https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Reddit-Content-Policy (platform-published; canonical doc)
- **Explicit non-find:** Reddit publishes **no engagement-rate or posting-frequency benchmarks**, and no reputable named third-party study of dealership-level Reddit performance could be cited at time of writing. **No Reddit engagement numbers are stated here.** House rules for the pilot:
  - **No hard-selling.** A dealership account that posts offers/links in local or car subreddits will be removed and can hurt the store's reputation.
  - Post/comment **as a real participant** (a person, not a billboard), answer questions, share genuinely useful local info, and let a mention of the store be earned, not pushed.
  - Before a store posts on Reddit at all, the audit must confirm **subreddit fit** — which subreddits exist for the store's market, and what their rules say.

## 6. YouTube format benchmarks

- **Platform-published (verified live 2026-08):** YouTube's Help documents the upload workflow, including **thumbnails** ("The image viewers will view before choosing your video") and **playlists** (organizing videos). Both are standard levers for a dealership channel.
  - Source: YouTube Help, "Upload videos," https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57407 (platform-published; verified live 2026-08)
- **Shorts vs long-form:** YouTube positions Shorts as the discovery format and long-form as the depth format. **Explicit non-find:** a current platform-published statistic on Shorts reach/performance could not be re-verified at time of writing (the previously cited YouTube announcement URL was not retrievable on 2026-08). **No Shorts view-count statistic is stated here.** House rule for the pilot:
  - Use **Shorts for discovery** (new arrivals, quick service tips, walkarounds — vertical, ≤60s) and **long-form for value** (detailed walkarounds, comparisons, service explainers, customer stories).
  - Consistent rhythm: **no unexplained gap > 2–3 weeks**; Shorts can be weekly, long-form monthly to start.
  - Every video gets a **real thumbnail and a descriptive title** with local keywords; group videos into **playlists** (e.g., "New Arrivals," "Service Tips").
