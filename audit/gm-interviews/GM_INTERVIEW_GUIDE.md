# GM ONE-ON-ONE INTERVIEW GUIDE — Month 1 Audit

**Status:** INTERNAL — working tool for the operator (Shannon McNeil). Not client-facing.
**Last updated:** 2026-08-12
**Owner:** Bright Matter LLC agency operations team (Audit & Research Analyst)
**Companion file:** `BASELINE_QUESTIONNAIRE.md` (the numbered question set this guide runs)
**Applies to:** 5 rooftops × 1 GM each. Store 03 and Store 04 share a GM (James Galuszka); see §6.

---

## 0. What this is and who runs it

1. The operator (Shannon McNeil) runs every interview. No agent ever talks to a GM, a store employee, or anyone at Greenway. Agents prepare; the operator conducts.
2. Purpose of the interview: fill the six audit buckets (accounts and access, performance baselines, reputation, search presence, competitive benchmarking, operations workflow) and the `CLIENT.md` §8 checklist with answers that only the store can give.
3. Target length: about 40 minutes per rooftop. A GM reads on a phone between customers. Keep it tight, ask the numbered questions in order, and write answers as you go.

## 1. Before the interview — checklist

Work through these in order before each session.

1. **Review `expansion-signals.md` with the operator (team lead does this).** Pull the rows for the store you are about to meet. The operator decides what, if anything, gets raised in that meeting. Nothing is pitched, proposed, or priced in any meeting, ever (`OPERATING_RULES.md` §2.4). If the store has no rows, nothing is raised.
2. **Re-read the store's audit workbook.** Open `audit/workbook/store-XX/audit.csv` (and the matching tab in `WORKBOOK.md`) and read every bucket. Also read `audit/workbook/BLOCKERS.md`. Know exactly which blockers this interview can unblock: Bucket 1 accounts and access, Bucket 2 goals and baselines, Bucket 3 reputation ownership, Bucket 5 competitor set, Bucket 6 operations workflow.
3. **Bring the store's observed public data.** Use the table in §7. It holds only what the team observed on 2026-08-12 (Google Business Profile rating, last observed owner post, address, phone, website). Use entries to confirm and to frame observations neutrally, never as a judgment. Example: "We noticed the Google listing posts went quiet after June 18. Is that a fair read?"
4. **Open one copy of `BASELINE_QUESTIONNAIRE.md` per rooftop.** Fill the header fields (store, GM, date, format) before you start.
5. **Confirm logistics.** Time, place, in person or by phone, and how much time the GM can give. For Stores 03 and 04, agree with the GM whether it is two separate interviews or one joint session. Either format works; the questionnaire is completed once per rooftop either way (§6).
6. **Set your own reminders before walking in.** Hold sensitive findings for the one-on-one. Frame reputation as absence, never a complaint list (`OPERATING_RULES.md` §9–10). Ask the questions in order. Write the answers down as they are given.

## 2. Interview flow

1. **Open (2 minutes).** Thank the GM. State the purpose in one line: "We are building a baseline of how the store shows up online so the 6-month pilot has a starting point, and we need your answers to a short set of questions." State the length: "About 40 minutes, and I will keep it moving." If the GM has only 20 minutes, run Parts 1–3 (accounts, goals, workflow) fully and the rest briefly; those three are the Month 1 priorities.
2. **Rapport (1–2 minutes).** One genuine question: how is business, what is busy in the store this month. This is not small talk for its own sake; it tells you which part of the business (sales, service, used) matters most to the store right now, which Part 2 answers confirm.
3. **Main questions (30–35 minutes).** Run `BASELINE_QUESTIONNAIRE.md` in order, part by part, question by question. Do not jump around. If the GM answers a later question early, note it and move on when you reach it.
4. **Close (2–3 minutes).** Recap the three most important things you heard. State what happens next: answers get logged, access requests get routed through the operator, and the store will see the audit findings one-on-one before anything is shared in a group setting. Confirm the points of contact from Part 6. Thank the GM for the time.

| **Total** | | **~40 min** | Joint Store 03/04 session: budget up to 60 min |

Handling interruptions (a GM will be interrupted):

1. Pause the moment the GM is pulled away. Note the current question number on the questionnaire.
2. When they return, re-read the last question they answered, then continue from the next number.
3. Never chase a tangent past two minutes. If the GM is telling a story that matters, note the thread in the notes template under follow-ups and steer back.
4. If the GM offers to delegate the interview to someone else (marketing manager, internet manager), accept it if it is the person who actually runs the accounts; that person is often the better source for Parts 1 and 3. Mark the respondent's name and role in the header.

## 4. Handling sensitive findings

Sensitive findings are Reddit complaints, fee-transparency problems, negative review patterns, legacy brand issues, and the staff-driven review pattern (`OPERATING_RULES.md` §4, §9–11). This one-on-one is exactly where they belong. Rules for the room:

1. **Never read a complaint list.** If the topic of what customers say comes up, frame it as absence: "Shoppers are not finding the store in the conversation" or "the store is not present where customers talk." The GM hears that as fixable. "Twelve people are angry on Reddit" reads as an attack.
2. **Confirm, do not confront.** If a sensitive finding exists for this store (see §7 and the workbook), raise it as a question, not an accusation. Example: "We noticed the Google listing posts stopped in mid-June. Is that a tooling change or a staffing thing?" The GM will usually explain.
3. **Named-staff review pattern.** At every rooftop, named individual staff drive nearly all positive reviews. If the GM raises reviews, you may ask one neutral question: "Is the store comfortable with its reputation living mostly with individual team members, or is there interest in building the store brand itself?" Do not push further in this meeting.
4. **If the GM raises a complaint or a problem, listen and note it.** Write it in the notes template under "Sensitive, one-on-one only." Do not promise fixes, do not promise timing, do not take action in the room.
5. **If the GM asks about paid ads, pricing, or expanding the scope**, answer plainly that the pilot is organic social only and that the store's interest is noted; the operator will pick the conversation back up at the right time. No proposal, no price, no commitment. Note the interest in the notes; it is a signal, not a pitch.
6. **Group settings never carry these.** Nothing sensitive from this meeting goes into any group GM meeting or the COO rollup. The COO readout carries absence and freshness gaps only (`GM_KPI_FRAMEWORK.md` §5.8).

## 8. After the interview — steps

Do these within 24 hours of the session.

1. **Log answers into the store's audit workbook.** Update `audit/workbook/store-XX/audit.csv` (and the matching tab in `WORKBOOK.md`): replace BLOCKER rows the GM answered, add findings to the right buckets, and record the interview as the source with its date. If a question was not answered, the cell stays TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil.
2. **Tick the `CLIENT.md` §8 checklist.** Mark the items the interview answered (handles, ownership, goals, workflow, contacts, approval routing, start date). Items still open stay unchecked and TO VERIFY.
3. **Update `expansion-signals.md`.** Add a row only for real, specific, observed openings from this session, with supporting evidence and the capability implied. No pricing, no proposals. The team lead reviews the log with the operator before the next GM one-on-one.
4. **Feed `engagement-state.md`.** Note the interview as complete, and clear the blockers the interview unblocked (owner: Shannon McNeil for store data). Blockers that need action, such as access grants via Shawn Vink, go on the open list.
5. **Route follow-ups to the operator.** Anything the GM committed to, any access request, and any second session needed goes through Shannon McNeil. No agent contacts the store.
6. **Flag to the team lead.** Anything that changes the audit plan (a vendor revealed in Part 1, an orphaned account in Q8, a compliance rule in Q24) goes in the notes and to the team lead, so the content strategist and reporting analyst build against what the interview actually found.

## 9. Rules recap

1. The operator is the only client-facing voice. No agent contacts any GM, employee, or vendor at Greenway.
2. Every file in this package is INTERNAL. Nothing from it goes to the client without the operator's approval (approval pipeline: `WORKFLOW.md`).
3. No pitching, no pricing, no proposals, in any meeting, ever. Openings go to the expansion log and the operator decides what gets raised, when, and with which GM.
4. Sensitive findings stay one-on-one. Reputation is framed as absence, never as a complaint list.
5. Nothing is invented. Answers come from the interviews; anything not answered stays TO VERIFY — request from client via Shannon McNeil.
6. Store-by-store, never group templates. Store 03 and Store 04 keep separate answers even in a joint session.
