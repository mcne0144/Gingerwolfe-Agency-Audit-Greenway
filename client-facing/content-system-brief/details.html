Findings

What we learned from your Orlando stores.

We pulled every top post from Kia North, Kia West and Kia East on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, then read 739 comments and 21 transcripts to see what the audience actually responded to.

The pattern is consistent. The subject of a post barely matters. The format decides everything.

The clearest proof is on Kia West. A lease offer posted as a static image drew two likes.

The same kind of offer, delivered by a salesperson on camera who opened with "if you buy fast food every week, you can afford this car," drew 120,000 plays and 1,500 saves. Same store, same month, same offer.

Kia North built a 35,000-follower account without a single personality. Their engine is short demos of one feature, framed as a question. "Would you use this?" reached 731,000 people and was saved 5,400 times, which means people sent it to the person they shop with.

Kia East has 590 TikTok followers and posts in Spanish that reach 36,000 people. That is sixty times its audience, repeatedly. Nobody else in the group is serving those buyers.

What the audience told us

  • People follow a person, not a store. "I don't know what state you're in but I followed."
  • Owners learn their car's features from TikTok, not the manual, and they ask for the trim and year on every demo.
  • The same objection appears on every winning post, so we answer it once, well, instead of arguing in captions.
  • Every winner starts talking in the first half second. No logo, no music over the voice, no "welcome to."
  • Reach is national. Local conversion is a separate job, and we treat it as one.

Hero formats are where the biggest reach lives and need a willing person. Workhorse formats need no personality and are the reliable weekly baseline.

Inventory graphics, hours notices and holiday posts still get published, but they are utility, not content, and we do not count them.

The weekly system

The full week, step by step.

  1. We send the brief on Monday

    A two-page brief per store: which format, who is on camera, three shots to capture, the caption already written, the vehicle confirmed on the lot. It comes from the format library plus what we know about your store.

  2. Your person shoots it on a phone

    One capture, one take where possible, native sound. The brief tells them what to say in the first three seconds. Fifteen minutes on the lot, not a production day.

  3. We cut it for every channel

    One capture becomes the TikTok, the Instagram Reel, the Facebook post, and a YouTube Short. We adjust the captions per channel. Nothing is reshot and nothing is posted once.

  4. We answer the comments and route the leads

    We answer questions about trim, year and price. Anyone showing intent goes to your store the same way messages reach you today, with a copy kept in Sprout.

  5. The scorecard tells us what to make next

    What performed re-ranks the formats for next week. We build your scorecard on your own numbers, against your own baseline, not against Orlando.

The scorecard

What we measure, and what we will not claim.

Each store's scorecard starts from its own baseline in month one. We track reach, saves and shares (the signal that people are passing content along), comments and messages, follower growth, and the share of posts that hit their format's benchmark.

We will not report a viral number as a local result. A post that reaches a million people reached people in Malta and Arizona. The scorecard separates reach from local conversation and local messages, so nobody mistakes one for the other.

Paid promotion is outside this pilot. We build every post so it is ready to boost the day that changes, because the formats that win organically are the ones worth paying to extend.

What we need from each store

  • One confirmed account per channel, with duplicates retired or merged.
  • One person willing to be on camera, even for the workhorse formats.
  • Fifteen minutes of lot time per capture, a few times a week.
  • Confirmation that any vehicle or offer we feature is real and available before it posts.
  • A yes or no from the general manager on the one-on-one items above.
